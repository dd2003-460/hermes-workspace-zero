LLM judges are default in automated evaluation, yet their reliability on the formalizable core — where the correct answer is a numeric value or spec-constrained quantity — has not been measured against an exact reference. We measure it. On 67 real model outputs across 10 tasks, a 27b LLM judge under three prompt variants emits unparseable output on 52–84% of checkpoints (Wilson 95% CI excluding 0), and on the checkpoints it does parse its verdicts disagree with a mechanical judge at ≈50% — indistinguishable from a coin flip (exact binomial p = 0.79–0.90). A mechanical judge (symbolic comparison or spec-driven regex) fails on 0/67 by construction. We isolate the mechanism: complexity lengthens the judge's reasoning chain, exhausting max_tokens and truncating the verdict — not attention dilution; prompt phrasing spans 31.4pp across variants, V3 best at 52.2%. We certify the task set against guessing via a model-agnostic bound (P(all-correct by guess) ≤ 3.3×10⁻⁵) and show prompt optimization trades one checkpoint dimension for another. The contribution is the *reference*: on the formalizable core a mechanical judge is exact on calc checkpoints, free, and reproducible, and is the appropriate default there, with LLM judgment reserved for the non-formalizable residual.
