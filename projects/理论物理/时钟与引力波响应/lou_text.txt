Abstract
Abstract
Gravitational wave observation offers a new window for understanding the uni-
verse. In the low-frequency band, space-based gravitational wave detectors such as
LISA, Taiji, and TianQin are being actively developed, while in the lower nHz band,
pulsar timing array (PTA) technique is utilized. In recent years, gravitational wave de-
tection schemes based on optical lattice clock (OLC) have attracted attention due to their
high precision and potential sensitivity advantages. This thesis investigates the detection
capability of an OLC detector network for the stochastic gravitational wave background
(SGWB), focusing on the relationship between the Overlap Reduction Function (ORF)
and the geometric configuration of the detector network.
This thesis first analyzes the invariance of the ORF of an OLC detector network un-
der configuration transformations. It points out that, in addition to trivial equivalences
such as swapping detector labels, global translations, and global orthogonal transfor-
mations, a nontrivial transformation that leaves the ORF modulus invariant is discov-
ered by analyzing the complex structure of the integrand. This transformation involves
swapping the positions of the emitting and receiving lasers for each detector. Under the
condition of equal arm lengths, both the real and imaginary parts of the ORF remain
unchanged before and after the transformation. Furthermore, the geometric condition
corresponding to a zero imaginary part of the ORF is discussed, and the corresponding
configuration characterization is provided. Subsequently, numerical comparisons of the
ORF are conducted for several typical coplanar, equal-arm configurations. The analysis
focuses on the variation of the ORF with the detector angle, arm length, and detector sep-
aration in PTA-like configurations and isosceles trapezoid configurations. The results
indicate as follows: In PTA-like configurations, the peak frequency of the ORF curve is
inversely proportional to the detector arm length; for isosceles trapezoid configurations,
as the distance between the detector centroids increases, the ORF peak value decreases;
the variation of the ORF peak value with the angle for PTA-like configurations and
small-separation isosceles trapezoid configurations generally follows the angular corre-
lation described by the Hellings ⚶Downs curve. In contrast, large-separation isosceles
trapezoid configurations exhibit variation characteristics different from the Hellings ⚶
Downs curve. Finally, a specific OLC detector network configuration is designed, its
sensitivity is analyzed, and compared with the sensitivities of laser interferometer grav-
itational wave detectors such as LISA, Taiji, and TianQin.
Key Words: Optical Lattice Clock, Stochastic Gravitational Wave Background, Over-
lap Reduction Function, Detector Network Configuration
