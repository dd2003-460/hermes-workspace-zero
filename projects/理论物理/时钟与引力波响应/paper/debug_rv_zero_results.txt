================================================================================
PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

Why R^V = 0 for planar detectors:

1. Mirror/Parity Symmetry: A planar detector (all satellites in one plane) has
   reflection symmetry. Under reflection across the detector plane:
   - The + polarization is unchanged (even parity)
   - The x polarization flips sign (odd parity)
   - The detector response M(th,phi) transforms such that M(theta) -> M(pi-theta)
     with F_x flipping sign

2. Cross-correlation of two identical-planar detectors:
   Y_V = F_+1 * F_x2 - F_x1 * F_+2
   Under reflection: F_+ -> F_+, F_x -> -F_x
   Y_V -> -(F_+1 * F_x2 - F_x1 * F_+2) = -Y_V
   The integrand is odd -> integrates to zero

3. This holds for:
   - Self-correlation (identical detector): always zero by symmetry
   - Cross-correlation between any two coplanar detectors: always zero
   - The result is independent of inclination angle (as long as both detectors
     share the same reflection plane geometry)

4. The Michelson formalism (arm_a - arm_b) is equivalent to CW-CCW difference,
   which is zero for equilateral triangles. The E-variable (CW sum of 3 arms)
   gives a different total response, but the chiral ratio R^V/R^I still vanishes
   because the parity symmetry is a property of the antenna patterns, not the
   specific interferometer topology.

5. Nonzero R^V requires:
   - Detectors with different reflection planes (e.g., LISA at 60 deg inclination
     crossed with a detector at a DIFFERENT inclination breaks the common parity)
   - Even then, with correct antenna pattern computation, R^V = 0.
   - True chiral sensitivity requires either:
     a) Non-planar detector configuration
     b) Multiple interferometers combined with different arm orientations
     c) Time-varying arm lengths breaking reflection symmetry
